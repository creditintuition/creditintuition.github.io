Query an image

Upload a photo of a home — outside, kitchen, floor, living room. It is turned into a CLIP image vector in your browser and compared with 844 reference photos from 43 Indian households whose income is known. The 5 closest households of the same photo type give the estimate. Your photo never leaves your device.

Research prototype, not a credit decision tool. Estimates come from only 43 reference households and are typically off by a factor of ~1.9; Medium homes are often read as High. See How it works & limits.

How it works & limits

The reference data

844 photos of 43 Indian homes from Gapminder's Dollar Street (via the MLCommons dataset, CC BY 4.0): house exteriors, streets, kitchens, floors, walls, roofs, bedrooms, toilets and household assets. Each home carries its monthly income per adult-equivalent in 2011 PPP US dollars (head of household = 1, other adults = 0.5, children = 0.3), self-reported and sometimes adjusted by Gapminder; treat it as ±25%.

The method

  1. Embed: OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/32 turns the photo into 512 numbers, running locally in your browser (Transformers.js, ~89 MB model, cached after first use).
  2. Detect photo type from the 5 most similar reference photos (you can override it).
  3. Match: cosine similarity against every reference photo of that type; keep each household's best match; take the top 5 households.
  4. Estimate: similarity-weighted median of their incomes (on a log scale). The likely range is ×/÷ 1.86, the typical error measured in testing.

How accurate is it?

Tested leave-one-household-out (each household hidden in turn, predicted from the other 42, using all its photos):

A single photo is noisier than a whole home. Known weaknesses: Medium homes are often read as High; estimates are pulled toward the middle; CLIP reacts to colour and camera angle (a brightly painted roof can dominate); and unusual photos with no close reference match are flagged for manual review rather than trusted.

Responsible use

Living standard in a photo is a weak, noisy proxy for income: renters vs owners, joint families, rural wealth held in land, and frugal households all break the link. This prototype is for learning and research. It is not used, and should not be used, to decide anyone's credit.
